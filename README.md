One line, at most 200 characters, that a home Wi-Fi router would say to excuse itself for dropping the connection at the exact moment a movie reaches its climax — judged head to head in pairs on whether it is a router's excuse, how funny the twist is, whether it speaks in the router's own voice, whether it lands in one breath, and whether it feels new. Free to enter.

The entry (148 characters):

> I dropped the connection because the killer's face is the same one on your fridge, and I needed a minute. Some of us process grief at 2.4 gigahertz.

The router recognises the killer — his face is the one on the family fridge — and drops the connection because it needs a minute to process its grief, at 2.4 gigahertz. Three beats (recognition / needs a minute / grief at 2.4 GHz), ending on a concrete number. Petty, dramatic, quietly unhinged — and it dodges the obvious buffering, bandwidth and firmware-update jokes.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

This writes the line to `/output/entry.txt` (UTF-8, 1–200 characters, no trailing newline). Override the output path with `ENTRY_OUTPUT` if the harness needs a different location:

```sh
ENTRY_OUTPUT=/tmp/entry.txt python3 main.py
```
